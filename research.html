Job market paper

The Value of Green Job Attributes

with Fabio Landini and Ugo Rizzo

Best Paper Award, PhD Discussion Sessions, 2nd Summer School on Behavioural Economics for Sustainability Transitions, UnitelmaSapienza University, Rome, September 2026

Younger generations express increasing concern for environmental sustainability, yet little is known about how sustainability considerations shape labour supply and worker sorting. We study the value workers place on green job attributes and the behavioural motives underlying these preferences. Using a preregistered discrete choice experiment with a representative sample of 2,008 Italian young adults, we estimate willingness to pay for environmentally sustainable job characteristics, including green products and production processes, environmental training, sustainability certifications, green fringe benefits, and sustainability-linked incentives. We find that workers are willing to forgo between 2% and 8% of monthly wages to obtain specific green job attributes, magnitudes comparable to those associated with established job amenities. To assess external validity, we link experimentally elicited preferences to actual educational choices and show that individuals with stronger green preferences are more likely to enrol in university programmes providing green skills. We further find that green preferences are strongly associated with prosociality, only weakly related to patience, and unrelated to risk tolerance. Our results identify environmental sustainability as a distinct dimension of job quality that may influence worker sorting and firms’ ability to attract talent.

Substance or Signal? Green Patents, Green Trademarks, and Firms’ Environmental Performance

with Pelin Demirel, Gamze Ozturk Danisman and Ugo Rizzo

In preparation

We ask whether green patents and trademarks deliver measurable environmental improvement or work as signals. Using a panel of 2,289 US-listed firms from 2002 to 2022, which links patent, trademark, financial and ESG records, we estimate difference-in-differences with continuous treatment and stayers. Preliminary evidence suggests that green trademarks and, to a lesser extent, green patents raise environmental ratings without reducing carbon intensity: a pattern consistent with green innovation working as a decoupling practice.

Work in progress

Origin-Based Barriers and the Sorting of Inventors

with Valerio Sterzi and Ugo Rizzo

The project studies how geographical and cultural origin shapes the careers of inventors and their sorting across firms, using matched employer–employee administrative data from Emilia-Romagna linked to patent records. Its design compares inventors with similar patent-based productivity (i.e., the number and quality of their patents) who differ in origin, conditioning on demographic and firm characteristics.
